# How a paid bucket can reach a range's ball machine

Researched 2026-11-19.

Most commercial ball dispensers take payment the same way underneath. Each payment device (coin or token acceptor, card reader, PIN pad) closes a contact on one of a few low-voltage "pay channels", and the machine dispenses a set number of balls for each pulse. Range Servant, the brand most likely to be at an Australian range, documents this clearly. Its BA-16 controller has six pay channels at 12 V DC, and a closure to ground of at least 100 ms dispenses that channel's bucket. A RangeOps payment box (idea 11) is therefore technically simple: it closes a contact. The hard parts are not technical. The terminals sit inside a cabinet that also carries 230 V. The maker says alterations need its consultation or the warranty is affected. A box RangeOps supplies to a range brings Australian compliance work (RCM marking for radio and EMC). And no maker publishes an open API. Integrations by software (PINs issued by a POS, Range Servant's RSConnect "API-Integration", Select Pi, eRange) all exist, but each needs credentials from the vendor. Under NSW's definition, wiring at 50 V AC or less is outside "electrical installation", so connecting to a 12 V pay channel does not look like licensed electrical wiring work. Opening a mains cabinet is still a job for the maker's technician. For the pilot, keep the staff hand-over. Model the bucket code so a machine PIN or a pay-channel pulse can be added later, and decide only once the pilot range's exact machine is known.

## 1. Range Servant

**The machine side is well documented.** The English Ultima manual covers the BA-16 control system, which is "intended for use with Range Servant's Ultima and RS series" ([Ultima manual](https://www.rangeservant.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Manual-Dispenser-Ultima-ENG.pdf), §5.1.1). It "offers up to six separate payment inputs (channels) and allows for ball amounts to be adjusted for each channel". It also offers connection to "smart payment systems" and to RSConnect. The low-voltage connections table (§5.1.2) gives:

- **P1–P6**: "Pay channels for payment devices. One pulse will dispense the amount of balls set in the Menu system. For a pulse to be recognised it needs to be at least 100ms and close to Ground (G)."
- **G**: ground. **+**: a 12 V DC supply, "A maximum of 2Amps is allowed for external devices."
- **D**: a dispense output that "Goes low when dispensing". It is wired to the red front light and could tell a payment box that the machine is dispensing.
- Ball counts per channel are set in the menu (Pay 1 to Pay 6, from 1 to 255 balls per payment). The control voltage is 12 V DC. Mains is 230/115 V 50/60 Hz (§1.6).

In short, the BA-16 takes a dry contact or open-collector closure to ground. A coin acceptor, a token switch, a Nayax-style reader relay or an ESP32 driving a relay or optocoupler would all look the same to it. The older MicroLine-8 with the BA-99 controller has the same idea with three channels, P1–P3, also at 12 V DC ([BA-99 manual](https://www.rangeservant.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/MicroLine-8-BA-99-E.pdf), §3.2.1).

The same board carries mains terminals (L, N, motor and water valve outputs, and an accessory mains output). The manual warns: "Only persons with sufficient knowledge and certifications are allowed to handle any Mains voltage connections! Always consult a certified electrician before attempting any work on these connections!" (§5.1.2). It also says "Never touch the circuit board or other electrical components in the electrical cabinet" (§2.3).

**Payment options sold by Range Servant.** The [payment solutions page](https://www.rangeservant.com/payment-solutions/) lists coins and tokens, card and contactless, [Range Servant Pay](https://www.rangeservant.com/rangeservantpay/) ("powered by Sweetspot", app-based), and [SOLO](https://www.rangeservant.com/solo/), a Mifare card system sold fitted or as replacement kits. GolfMore appears in the menu without a description. No page mentions QR, PIN or Nayax by name. The Ultima manual notes that several payment methods can run at once, "for example combining token/coin or token/coin/card" (§3.2.2), and that "If the machine is card-operated it must stand under cover for the warranty to apply" (§4.2).

**How third parties get credit to a Range Servant machine:**

- **Pulse on a pay channel.** This is the documented hardware route above, and any payment device can use it.
- **PIN or card credit through Range Servant's server.** Vermont Systems' RecTrac POS talks to "the Ranger Servant Server" with an XML file (host, port, register number). The server either returns a single-use PIN printed on the receipt or credits a member's Range Servant card. "The Range Management System software handles the expiring of unused PINs", and cancellations deduct the value ([RecTrac doc](https://vermont-systems.helpjuice.com/en_US/topic-docs/td_range-ball-solutions-in-rectrac_help)). The same document lists "Select PI (Newest Range Servant API)", which takes a JSON request and needs a tenant ID, username, passkey and vendor role.
- **RSConnect.** A router-based dashboard for statistics, low-ball alerts and health. It lists "API-Integration: This optional feature will enable integration with Third Party Solutions" (Ultima manual §4.3.1). No API documentation is published. Onboarding starts by scanning a QR code on the router, after which Range Servant support makes contact (§4.3.4).
- **Other POS vendors.** ESP's Elite system issues "PIN/Barcode from the electronic point of sale" or tops up a contactless card. It names Range Servant and European Golf Machinery as dispenser makers it works with ([ESP](https://ww2.e-s-p.com/products/driving-range-integration/)). TrackMan lists a Range Servant ball dispenser integration ([TrackMan](https://support.trackmangolf.com/hc/en-us/articles/42045428442651-Integrations-List-of-Available-Integrations)).

**Published API or partner programme:** none found. Every integration found goes through credentials Range Servant or Select Pi issue to the POS vendor. Getting an API key would mean approaching Range Servant, or its Australian distributor, once a range commits.

**Australia.** Range Servant's [worldwide page](https://www.rangeservant.com/worldwide/) lists Country Club International Pty Ltd (Carrum Downs, Victoria) for Australia and New Zealand. The [Country Club site](https://www.countryclub.com.au) sells Range Servant picker and washer parts and has a "Ball Dispensers & Payment Systems" collection. That collection could not be read (rate-limited), so which dispensers and payment systems it stocks is **unverified**.

**Warranty.** "Damage caused by the use of accessories and spare parts not approved by Range Servant or damage due to unauthorised technical changes are not covered by the warranty obligation" (Ultima manual §2.5). The full terms sit in Range Servant's General Terms and Conditions for Delivery, which are not published. The older BA-99 manual's one-year warranty "does not cover payment systems". It also does not apply to equipment "repaired or replaced by persons/companies not authorised by the manufacturer".

## 4. Generic retrofit routes

- **Pulse or contact emulation.** This is the most common route. A coin or token acceptor sends a pulse or closes a contact for each coin. Range Servant's own inputs want a closure to ground of at least 100 ms (above). Nayax, the largest cashless-reader maker, supports "Pulse" machines alongside MDB. Its pulse install guide offers three methods: a relay set ("Fast and easy, no electronics expertise required"), direct wiring ("requires expertise in electronics"), or wiring onto the coin mech's coin line. The reader runs on 10–24 V DC or 10–42 V AC ([Nayax pulse installation](https://nayax-u.nayax.com/article/pulse-hardware-installation-11793)). A relay with isolated contacts across Pn and G is the simplest and safest interface for a RangeOps box. It keeps the box's electronics isolated from the dispenser board.
- **MDB.** The Multi-Drop Bus / Internal Communication Protocol standard belongs to NAMA (US National Automatic Merchandising Association). Version 4.3 (2019) changed only Section 7, Cashless Devices ([Vending Market Watch](https://www.vendingmarketwatch.com/management/associations/national-associations/news/21094045/nama-releases-mdb-version-43)). **No ball dispenser was found that has an MDB port.** A UK range owner who bought a Nayax reader for a coin-only dispenser was told on the [Arduino forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/how-to-convert-a-golf-ball-dispenser-to-cashless/1022106) to look at the pay-channel terminals (G+P1–P3) instead. Treat MDB as irrelevant unless a range's machine has it.
- **ccTalk.** A serial protocol for coin and note validators, created by Coin Controls (later Money Controls, now Crane Payment Innovations) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CcTalk)). It sits between a validator and a machine controller, and was not found on any ball dispenser.
- **Tokens.** Range Servant sells 16 mechanical and 2 electronic token types (BA-99 manual §3.3.1), and many ranges run on tokens. A token acceptor with a metal-detect switch is just another pay-channel input.

## 5. What needs the maker or an electrician in NSW

This is a reading of public guidance, not legal advice. The legislation pages themselves (legislation.nsw.gov.au and AustLII) refused automated access, so the quotes below come from the NSW Government's own summary.

- **Electrical wiring work needs a licence.** "In NSW, you need an electrical licence or certificate before you can do any electrical wiring work regardless of the cost of the work or whether the work is residential, commercial, or industrial." Electrical wiring work is "the actual physical work of installing, repairing, altering, removing, or adding to an electrical installation". Unlicensed work breaches the Home Building Act 1989, with fines up to $22,000 for individuals and $110,000 for companies ([nsw.gov.au, Electrical work](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/licences-and-credentials/building-and-trades-licences-and-credentials/electrical)). The definitions come from the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2017.
- **ELV and plug-in equipment are outside "electrical installation".** The same page lists exclusions: "any electrical article connected to, and extending or situated beyond, any electrical outlet socket" and "any electrical equipment operating at not more than 50 volts alternating current or 120 volts ripple-free direct current". These are the extra-low voltage limits in AS/NZS 3000. Range Servant dispensers normally plug into a wall outlet (Ultima manual §5.1.2), and their pay channels run at 12 V DC. So wiring a RangeOps box to P1–P6 does not appear to be licensed "electrical wiring work".
- **Opening the machine is still not a DIY job.** The pay terminals share a board and cabinet with live 230 V terminals. Range Servant says only certified people should touch mains connections, never to touch the board, and to make alterations only in consultation with Range Servant (Ultima manual §2.3, §5.1.2). The practical rule: the maker's technician (through Country Club International in Australia) fits the wires, or at least approves the method. Anything that changes the machine's mains side, such as tapping 230 V to power the box, is out of bounds for RangeOps. Where the dispenser is hard-wired rather than plugged in, its supply wiring is an electrical installation and needs a licensed electrician.
- **Warranty.** Unauthorised technical changes are not covered (Ultima manual §2.5). On older machines, payment systems were never covered and work by unauthorised people voids the warranty (BA-99 manual). Range Servant's full Terms and Conditions for Delivery are not published.
- **A box RangeOps supplies (RCM, EESS, ACMA).**
  - *Electrical safety (EESS).* Equipment operating below 50 V AC or 120 V ripple-free DC is **not in scope** of the EESS. Nor is equipment used exclusively for commercial purposes. Out-of-scope gear must still be electrically safe under AS/NZS 3820 ([EESS not in scope](https://www.eess.gov.au/equipment/not-in-scope/)). The mains power supply is the in-scope part: EESS lists a "Power supply or charger" as Level 3. So power the box either from an off-the-shelf, RCM-marked plug pack, or from the dispenser's own +12 V output (2 A max, with maker approval). Never build a mains supply into the box. NSW runs its own scheme under the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act. The EESS overview lists Queensland, Victoria, WA and Tasmania as IGA signatories ([EESS about](http://www.eess.gov.au/about/about-the-eess/)), so how NSW recognises EESS registrations is **unverified**.
  - *Radio and EMC (ACMA).* An ESP32 box with Wi-Fi or Bluetooth is a radio transmitter. Its Australian supplier must meet ACMA's radiocommunications and EMC rules. That means registering as a "Responsible Supplier" on the national database (free for ACMA-only), keeping a compliance folder and applying the RCM ([EESS: ACMA use of RCM](https://www.eess.gov.au/rcm/acma-requirements-use-of-rcm/); [ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/know-what-you-must-do)). Pre-certified ESP32 modules make the testing easier, but RangeOps would likely still be the supplier of the finished box. Whether a single unit lent for a private pilot counts as "supply" is **unverified** and worth asking ACMA before shipping to a range.

## 6. Fallbacks without integration

- **Staff hand-over (the current plan).** The console shows the paid order and its bucket code. Staff check the code and hand over a basket, a token or a machine PIN. It works with any machine and any range.
- **Tokens bought in bulk.** Most dispensers take the range's own tokens. Staff swap an app bucket code for a token. Nothing to wire, and the range keeps its existing cash-up.
- **Pre-generated machine PINs reissued by the app.** The software exists but not as a plain list:
  - Range Star can "Print batch PINS for tournament play" ([Range Star](https://rangestargolf.com/LandingPage)).
  - GolfNext PINs can be single or multi-use with start and expiry dates, delivered as printed vouchers or SMS ([GolfNext](https://golfnext.com/driving-range)).
  - Range Servant PINs are generated per sale by its server for a POS. They are single-use and expire under the range management software's control ([RecTrac doc](https://vermont-systems.helpjuice.com/en_US/topic-docs/td_range-ball-solutions-in-rectrac_help)).
  - eRange PINs expire at midnight on the day of sale, which rules out stockpiling.

  No source shows a Range Servant feature for exporting a batch of PINs, so whether the pilot range could hand RangeOps a pre-generated list is **unknown**. If the range's system can print batch PINs, the app could reissue one per paid bucket. That fits the plan's "a bucket is a code or PIN" exactly. The cost is reconciling unused PINs with the range.

## Recommendation for the plan

**Decision 5 (how a paid bucket reaches the ball machine): keep the staff hand-over for the pilot, as planned.** Then integrate in this order once a range commits:

1. **Use the range's own PIN or QR system if it has one** (Range Servant via Select Pi or its POS link, eRange, Range Star, GolfNext). RangeOps would sell the bucket and show the machine's PIN. This needs vendor credentials or a batch-PIN export, but no hardware and no warranty risk. Trade-off: it depends on a vendor's goodwill. With Range Servant, the commercial route may run through Sweetspot, and nothing should imply a partnership with Sweetspot.
2. **Otherwise, idea 11 (the payment box).** It is technically sound and simpler than the plan implies. A QR reader and ESP32 drive a relay whose isolated contacts close Pn to G for about 200 ms per bucket, and the D output could confirm the dispense. On Range Servant it needs no protocol work. Trade-offs:
   - The box must be fitted with the maker's consent, preferably by its technician, or the warranty is at risk.
   - It must never touch mains. Power it from an RCM-marked plug pack or the dispenser's 12 V output.
   - A box supplied to a range brings ACMA RCM obligations.
   - Codes must be one-use and checked by the server (or signed and checked offline), because each pulse is free balls.

   Its 12 V wiring is outside NSW's definition of an electrical installation, so it does not need a licensed electrician. Do not build it before the pilot range's machine is known.
3. **Correct the source for "Japan shows another route".** Replace the Impress Watch link, which is about PLATTO GOLF and not a retrofit. Use the [Ome Riverside Park IoT retrofit](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000014.000011283.html) (QR by SMS at an existing dispenser) and, as the commercial equivalent, [GolfNext's retrofit door](https://golfnext.com/driving-range).

The data model needs no change. A bucket stays "a code or PIN", with a redemption method per range: `staff`, `machine_pin` or `pay_channel`.

- [ ] How do you cash up and reconcile today? Would app sales need to show up in the machine's own counts?
